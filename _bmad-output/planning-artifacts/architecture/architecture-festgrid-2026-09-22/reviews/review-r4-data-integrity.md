## Critical

### R4D-1. "Link only" members still collapse in Discovery, and a mixed group has no carrier
**Spine:** 2526-2543 (Rule 4: an unconfirmed group's effects are "the part of link and the Rule 13 Discovery policy"), 2678-2686 (Rule 12: 0.85 to 0.99 and tag joins "get the part-of link only"), 2840-2862 (Rule 13: a series is capped at one cover; the window partitions by `coalesce(g.id, e.id)` with `g.status = 'active'`, no `confirmed_at` term), 2964 (detector table: `confirmed_at` set for identical-core pairs, null for the rest, `[OPEN: carrier for a mixed group]`).
**Failure.** Three statements contradict each other. Rule 13 collapses by `kind = 'series'` and `status = 'active'`; `confirmed_at` is not in the partition. Rule 4 explicitly says an unconfirmed group still gets "the Rule 13 Discovery policy". So a `series_phrase` group created by a 0.90 pair or by a hashtag join (the two riskiest joins) hides its second member behind a k = 1 cover with no moderator step, which is exactly what option B said must not happen. The alternative reading (gate Rule 13 on `confirmed_at`) fails the other way: a group is one row, so one `confirmed_at` cannot say "A and B collapse, C is link only". On the owner's export, mixed groups exist: at the adopted bar there are 2 mixed components of 30 (3 of 57 if `unresolved` places are allowed), for example "Sports Station Buy 1 Get 1 Free Promo", five members that collapse, plus "Sports Station & Planet Sports Buy 1 Get 1 Promo" (0.85 to 0.99, link only); and "Bangun Pagi Bahagia" (account A0125): Tegal and Kudus score 1.00 (their place texts end in the city, so the city is stripped), Yogyakarta joins at 0.877 (its place "Institute France Indonesie, Lembaga Indonesia Prancis" does not end in the city, so the core keeps the word: link only), and Solo stays out at 0.83 and 0.79. Whichever of the two group-level values is chosen, either the link-only member is hidden or the collapsing members are not.
**Fix (minimal, one writer, one query).**
- The strength is a property of the **membership**, so it goes on the member row, next to `group_id`: `events.group_join group_join_enum null ('strong' | 'link')`, `CHECK ((group_id IS NULL) = (group_join IS NULL))`, written only by `group-membership.ts` in the same statement that sets `group_id`.
- `strong` = created or joined by a structural detector (`place_split`, `title_pattern`, `series_stem`, moderator) or by a `series_phrase` pair whose **core equals the group's stored `stem`** (weighted score 1.00). Compare to the stem, not "any active member": that removes the chain (a link-only member can never vouch for the next one). `link` = 0.85 to 0.99 and tag joins.
- Rule 13: `PARTITION BY coalesce(CASE WHEN e.group_join = 'strong' THEN g.id END, e.id)`. A `link` member is its own partition, shows its own card and the "part of" chip. `matching_count`, "also at N other places" and "+N more sessions" count strong members only.
- Rule 4 and Rule 9 inheritance read `group_join = 'strong'` AND `confirmed_at`, not `confirmed_at` alone. `confirmed_at` stays group-level (a moderator or a structural detector confirmed the group).
- Group-level counters (R4D-9): `member_count` (all active) and `strong_count`.
- Consistency queries (CI and nightly): (a) `(group_id IS NULL) <> (group_join IS NULL)`; (b) an active confirmed group with no strong member; (c) a `link` member whose group source is a structural detector (should not exist); (d) a `strong` `series_phrase` member whose core differs from `stem` (rename drift, R4D-5).
- Upgrade and downgrade of a member (a rename makes it identical, or breaks identity) go through the same writer and are journaled.

## High

### R4D-2. `eventCountry()` falls back to Indonesia, so the owner's AKMU example forms one group across four countries
**Spine:** 2701-2720 (area scope; "the Asia Tour (Osaka, Taipei, Kuala Lumpur) would not form one group"), AD-14 449-468 (leg (e)), AD-39 3175-3176 (gazetteer is Indonesia-only first).
**Failure (measured on the export).** `eventCountry()` = area-key country, else the country of a **trusted** pin, else a country **named in the text**, else `GEOAPIFY_DEFAULT_COUNTRY` (`id`). The gazetteer is Indonesia-only, so a foreign place has no area key. Leg (e) trusts a pin only if its country equals the expected country, and the expected country is the text-named country or the default; hence a correct pin in Taiwan or Japan is `untrusted` whenever the text does not name the country, and an untrusted pin "never counts". So step 2 (trusted pin) can never differ from steps 3 and 4: it is circular with (e). Result for "AKMU Asia Tour 2027 in Taipei / Osaka / Kuala Lumpur / Macau" (account A0066, places "Taipei Music Center", "Gorilla Hall Osaka", "Zepp Kuala Lumpur", "The Grand Hall, Grand Lisboa Palace..."): none names a country, all four resolve to `id`, the scope check passes, and they form one `series` group (same account, core "akmu asia tour 2027", score 1.00, places different). That is the opposite of the adopted decision and of the spine's own consequence sentence. The BWF events happen to work only because their place text is the bare country ("Egypt", "Denmark"). The error direction matters: wrongly separating two Indonesian events misses a group (status quo); wrongly joining two foreign events hides one behind the other.
**Fix.** `eventCountry()` returns a key or **unsettled** (NULL). The default country may be used only when no pin on the event carries a different `countryCode` (any confidence). Unsettled events attach to nothing (the CHECK in R4D-3 makes this structural). Cost: an Indonesian event with a wrong foreign pin (about 27% of pins by weight are outside the Indonesia box) and no area key and no country in its text stays ungrouped; size it with a Round 4 count (events with a pin whose `countryCode` differs from the default and no area key). This is a trade to put to the owner, not a silent default.

### R4D-3. The area scope has no stored country, no database invariant, and "a configuration change" is not true for stored groups
**Spine:** 2701-2720, 2860-2862 (read-time country in the partition), 2464-2490 (schema, no country column), AD-39 3407-3417 (`area_keys`, `area_leaf_key`, `area_source`, `area_status`).
**Failure.**
- *No column.* Rule 13 needs "the member's country key" inside one SQL window over up to K = 3,000 candidate rows. `eventCountry()` reads `area_keys` (events), the main schedule's `pin_trust` and `location_details` (schedules), free text (parsed against a country list in TypeScript) and an environment variable. It cannot be evaluated in the single SQL query the spine promises, and `area_keys` is an unordered-by-contract array (the country is "the root element" only if the writer always writes root-first). There is also no `event_groups` country, so attach has nothing to compare against except "some other member".
- *No invariant.* "One group = one country" is a cross-table property. A CHECK cannot express it; a consistency query only detects it. Drift sources: AD-39 Rule 2 compare-and-set (a later `stated` area replaces an `account` area and the country changes), a `pin_trust` re-derive (floor or gazetteer change), a correction or enrichment that rewrites `events.location`/`event_name` (`resolvers.ts:1482-1494`, `ENRICHABLE_EVENT_FIELDS`), a change of `GEOAPIFY_DEFAULT_COUNTRY`.
- *`GROUP_AREA_LEVEL`.* Changing `country` to `region` leaves every existing group spanning regions, the read-time partition starts splitting every such group's collapse while `member_count`, `place_count` and the group page stay group-wide. It is a re-key job, not a flag.
- *Merge, undo, split, dissolve* (all unspecified for a country mismatch): (a) `mergeEvents` winner inherits the loser's `group_id` (Rule 7) although winner and loser can have different countries (one of them is wrong by definition of a merge); (b) `undoEventMerge` restores `group_id` into an active group without comparing the member's current country; (c) dissolve-undo restores many members the same way; (d) a place split whose children land in two countries puts "every child in that same group" (AD-38 Rule 4); (e) a `writeEventArea()` that changes the country of a grouped event.
**Fix (database-enforced, verified on the scratch database).**
- `events.country_key text null`, written only by `writeEventArea()` (same writer, same compare-and-set rank, a `country_source` of `area | text | default`), NULL = unsettled (R4D-2). `event_groups.country_key text null`, set by the first attach, `UNIQUE (id, country_key)`.
- Hand-written in the migration: `FOREIGN KEY (group_id, country_key) REFERENCES event_groups (id, country_key) ON DELETE SET NULL (group_id)` plus `CHECK (group_id IS NULL OR country_key IS NOT NULL)`. Tested: an attach of a member with another country fails (FK), changing a grouped member's country fails unless `group_id` is cleared in the same statement, a NULL country with a group fails (CHECK), deleting the group keeps `country_key`, changing the group's country while it has members fails. This makes the invariant a constraint and the Rule 13 country term in the partition redundant (drop it, or keep it as a belt).
- Drizzle's `foreignKey({...}).onDelete('set null')` emits `ON DELETE SET NULL` for **both** columns, which would also null the event's `country_key`: the migration hand-edits the column list, and the schema builder still declares `set null` so `getTablesInDeleteOrder` (which skips set-null edges) does not throw on the `events`/`event_groups` cycle.
- Writer rules in `group-membership.ts`: attach, merge-inherit, undo-restore and dissolve-undo pre-check the country and **skip with a journaled reason**, never fail the merge (dedup must not block on a group, Rule 7); `writeEventArea()` calls `group-membership.detach(reason: 'country_changed')` before it changes the key. Lock order: see R4D-4.
- `GROUP_AREA_LEVEL` becomes the key level stored in `events.country_key` (rename `scope_key`); changing it is a migration step with an explicit re-split job, stated as such in the spine.
- Gate: the Step 5 switch of AD-39 Rule 7 must include `count(*) WHERE country_key IS NULL AND deleted_at IS NULL AND area_status IS NOT NULL` before any group detector flag is turned on.

### R4D-4. Serialisation: an organizer-only lock cannot serialise a cross-account attach, nothing is compare-and-set, and the group-count recompute can deadlock
**Spine:** 2794-2795 ("the lock is taken on the organizer alone, since a fuzzy key has no canonical string"), 2944-2952 (Rule 16 lock, nightly check), 2974-2975 (daily cap), 3008 (open question).
**Failure.**
1. *Two groups for one promo.* Events X (A0134) and Y (A0138) of the same promo are ingested by overlapping transactions (the key is the organizer, so they take different locks and do not wait). Neither sees the other's uncommitted event, so each ends ungrouped: a missed group that heals when the next member arrives. The corrupting case needs three or more: ungrouped X and Y are committed; C (account 3) and D (account 4) arrive concurrently; each takes its own organizer lock, each sees X and Y ungrouped, each inserts a group and runs `UPDATE events SET group_id = $g WHERE id IN (X, Y, self)`. The second UPDATE waits for the first's row locks, re-evaluates `id IN (...)` (which still matches), and overwrites. End state: G2 holds X, Y, D; G1 holds only C (`member_count` 1, dormant, with a name); the promo appears twice in Discovery for good, because attach runs only at ingestion. The spine's Precedence rule ("an event that already has a group is never moved by a lower creator") has no compare-and-set behind it.
2. *The nightly check is blind to this.* It flags "two active series groups with one key" (`organizer || stem`); a `series_phrase` group has no single organizer and no canonical stem equality, so there is no key to collide. The "merge groups" action (Rule 5) needs a moderator who is not reachable (BUG-069).
3. *Likelihood, honestly.* In prod the ingestor drains sequentially (`poll-and-drain-queue.ts`: `for ... await handleMessage`), one invocation per 5 minutes, so message 2 sees message 1 committed. The window is overlapping invocations, the sweep, the backfill, a moderator action, dev/staging event-source mapping (parallel batches), and the local inline fallback that fires `processIngestionJob` for every sibling without awaiting (`process-ai-job.ts:551-558`). Rare, but the outcome is permanent.
4. *Candidate retrieval is unspecified and truncating.* The matcher's candidate query is ±2 days with `LIMIT 20` on joined rows; the series join needs a GAP of 60 days over a fuzzy name. A truncated candidate list drops real members of a large family (Sports Station, 15 events in the largest component) and creates a second group by omission, the same end state as (1).
5. *Daily cap.* "A daily cap bounds suggestions" does not say whether a join counts or only a creation; a join that is blocked by the cap leaves the event ungrouped forever (no retry exists).
6. *Deadlock in the recount.* Rule 16 recomputes `member_count` "under a `FOR UPDATE` lock on the group row". If the writer first runs `UPDATE events SET group_id = $g` (the FK check takes `FOR KEY SHARE` on the group row, held to commit) and then locks the group row `FOR UPDATE`, two concurrent attaches into one group each hold KEY SHARE and each wait for the other's: a deadlock (PostgreSQL aborts one after `deadlock_timeout`; the SQS message retries). Documented lock behaviour, not run here.
**Answer to "what does a lock on a fuzzy key look like":** a canonical string does not exist, but a canonical **scope** does: the country (the group is single-country anyway, R4D-3). One lock per (detector, country) serialises every cross-account attach in that scope; at about 50 events a day the contention is nil. (Alternative with finer grain: lock each content token of the name, in sorted order from application code; any two names that score above 0.5 share at least two content tokens because of `MIN_SHARED_TOKENS`, so some lock is common. More locks, same effect, and the sorted order is mandatory or the tokens themselves deadlock.)
```sql
-- first statement of the attach (and of sweep, backfill and moderator attach); transaction-scoped
SET LOCAL lock_timeout = '5s';
SELECT pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended('group:series_phrase:' || $1, 0));   -- $1 = country key
-- then, in this order everywhere: group row, then member events by id
SELECT id FROM event_groups WHERE id = $2 FOR NO KEY UPDATE;
UPDATE events SET group_id = $2, group_join = $3, country_key = $4
 WHERE id = $5 AND group_id IS NULL RETURNING id;   -- 0 rows: lost the race, re-read and join the winner
```
Verified on the scratch database: `hashtextextended($1, 0)` types its parameter under `PREPARE` (the unnamed-statement path of `prepare: false`), the lock shows in `pg_locks` and is released at rollback. Pooler safety: a transaction-scoped lock lives and dies on the one backend the pooler pins for the transaction; AD-32 Rule 2's objection (a session lock, or an open transaction across a slow external call) does not apply because the ingestion transaction makes no external call (`process-ingestion-job.ts` 14-136: only DB statements). `lock_timeout` is needed because the container has `max: 1`, so a waiting transaction would otherwise hold the only connection until the Lambda timeout.
**Fix.**
- Lock key = (detector, country scope), lock first, `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` on the group row (it conflicts with other writers but not with FK `KEY SHARE`, so no deadlock and no blocking of unrelated inserts; the same mode for the event-row locks of AD-38 Rule 4 and AD-30 Rule 9, because `FOR UPDATE` blocks every favorite, calendar, schedule and report insert referencing that event).
- Every membership write is the conditional `UPDATE ... WHERE group_id IS NULL` (or `WHERE group_id = $expected`), checked for zero rows.
- The deterministic place-split group id gets the country in its UUID v5 input (R4D-11).
- Daily cap counts **creations** only; an event refused by the cap is retried by the nightly pass.
- The nightly pass is a reconcile, not a flag: re-run attach for ungrouped events from the last N days, and for each pair of active `series_phrase` groups in one country test the best member-pair score; join them with the same writer (identical-core pairs automatically, others listed).

**Spine:** 2734-2795 (score, weights, stem, name), 2765-2783 (tags), 2678-2686 (safety net), 2971-2975.
**Where the facts live (Question 2):**

**Failure.** `submitCorrection` rewrites `events.event_name` and `location` directly (`resolvers.ts:1482-1494`), and `enrichAndPromoteEvent` rewrites `eventName` and `location` from any later high-tier post (`set-event-primary-post.ts:144-153`, 412-420). Revalidation is also the nightly replay (reports, never silently repairs `link` joins).

- Give the nightly list a carrier now (an artifact page or a table the owner opens) or say plainly that the net is unobserved until IDEA-090.

### R4D-6. AD-30 Rule 7: the unordered-pair index, the expiry and the `superseded` status interact badly with the live insert and with AD-38 Rule 4
**Spine:** 1923-1925 (unordered unique index, "a re-run cannot re-queue a pair a moderator rejected"), 1954-1956 (insert bound younger than 7 days; "expires once either of its events has no open schedule"), AD-38 3258-3259 (`superseded` and re-evaluated). Code: `schema.ts:531-555` (enum `pending | accepted | rejected`, unique `(event_id, candidate_event_id)`), `process-ingestion-job.ts:121-133`.
**Failure (all four tested on the scratch database).**
1. *Index creation can abort the deploy.* If production holds one pair in both orientations, `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX ... (LEAST(event_id, candidate_event_id), GREATEST(...))` fails ("could not create unique index ... is duplicated"), and because drizzle's migrator runs the pending migrations in one transaction the whole batch rolls back. Probably clean (the live path orients new to existing), but unverified: add the pre-check `SELECT least(...), greatest(...), count(*) ... HAVING count(*) > 1`.
2. *Replacing the ordered constraint breaks every mid-tier ingestion.* The live insert names its target: `.onConflictDoNothing({ target: [eventId, candidateEventId] })`. After `DROP CONSTRAINT` of the ordered unique, PostgreSQL answers 42P10 ("no unique or exclusion constraint matching the ON CONFLICT specification") for every `mid` match, the ingestion transaction aborts and the message retries into the DLQ. With both present, the reverse orientation raises 23505 (not swallowed). Keep the ordered constraint, and have the sweep (the only possible reverse inserter) use a bare `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`; or change both inserters to bare.
3. *It forbids "closed as `superseded` and re-evaluated".* A second row for the unordered pair is impossible (the insert returned 0 rows); re-evaluation can only reopen the same row. Say so: `superseded` rows are reopened (status back to `pending`, new score, journaled), never re-inserted. The same applies to a pair that expired and later becomes valid again (a new date).
4. *Status values.* `superseded` and "expires" need enum values on an enum that already exists in production (`ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE`, unusable by a backfill in the same release, AD-39 Rule 7). Prefer derived expiry: `pending AND (either event deleted, merged, or without an open schedule)` as a predicate, no new value, no writer, no interaction with `undoEventMerge` (which sets a suggestion back to `pending`, step 9). Today's expiry wording covers only "no open schedule"; a pending candidate whose event was just merged or deleted by the exact-repeat merge stays actionable and `resolveSuggestedEventMatch` ACCEPT then throws `EventMergeInvalidStateError`.
5. *The insert bound grows the queue without limit.* Only pending rows younger than 7 days count, so the sweep adds up to 50 rows per run while rows older than 7 days stay `pending`, unexpired if their events still have open schedules; with no moderator working the queue (0 of 147 resolved) the list grows 50 per run.

### R4D-7. Exact-repeat merge by `system-dedup` automates a human operation that has no locks, no correction transfer and a date-only schedule match
**Spine:** 1947-1951 (exact repeats merged by the sweep), 1919-1929 (system actor, union-find), 1990-1998 (Rule 9 amendment); code `merge-events.ts`.
**Failure and size.** On the owner's export: 227 same-account pairs with the same normalized name, `isSamePlace` = same (author's check) and overlapping dates form 63 clusters over 174 events, so the first sweep soft-deletes about 111 events in one unattended batch (195 of the pairs have identical date ranges, 32 differ). Each merge is journaled and undoable, but:
- *No locks.* `mergeEvents` reads winner, loser and schedules without any row lock (`merge-events.ts:33-61`, 87-88). The Rule 9 amendment adds `FOR UPDATE` to enrichment only. A live post enriches the loser (schedule inserted, `event_posts` link added, possibly a promotion) between the merge's read and its soft delete: the new schedule and link sit on a soft-deleted event and never reach the winner. A human moderator rarely hits this; a sweep over 63 clusters while the drain runs does.
- *Corrections are not transferred.* `mergeEvents` repoints favorites, calendar additions, reports, `event_posts` and aliases, but not `corrections` (`corrections.event_id` is untouched; applied corrections protect fields through `getProtectedFields(eventId)`). An applied moderator correction on the loser vanishes and the winner's next enrichment may overwrite the same field. Add a precondition for the automatic path: refuse (list, do not merge) when either event has an applied or pending correction or a pending report.
- *Date-only schedule match.* Step 3 maps a loser schedule to a winner schedule by start date (`findScheduleByStartDate`), and the Rule 9 amendment adds the place. For an exact repeat the place is equal by construction, so a second session of the same activity on the same date at another time (separately posted, same name, same place) is treated as the same occurrence and dropped from view. Not measurable on the export (times are not in it); make the match `(start date, start time, place)` for the automatic path or require equal start times.
- *Group state and undo.* Rule 7 adds "one more column" to the merge journal. Two prior states are needed (winner's `group_id`/`group_join` because it may have inherited, loser's), plus the repointed anchors. With R4D-3 the inheritance is pre-checked for country and for `group_join`.
- *The system user.* `event_merges.performed_by_moderator_id` is `NOT NULL` referencing `users`; `users.email` is `NOT NULL UNIQUE`. The seeded row must not have `role = 'moderator'` (moderator alert fan-out would email it), needs a reserved non-routable address, and `seed.ts` clears every table except `reports` (`getTablesInDeleteOrder(['reports'])`) so a reseeded dev database loses it. The group-conflict "moderator notification" of Rule 7 has no carrier, so the conflict is silently dropped.
- *Hard delete of a winner.* `event_merges.winner_event_id`, `favorites.event_id` and `calendar_additions.event_id` all cascade: `deleteEventPermanently(winner)` erases the journal and every repointed favorite and calendar entry of the losers merged into it, leaving the losers soft-deleted with `merged_into_event_id` nulled. Pre-existing, but 111 automatic merges make a moderator hitting a winner likely. Refuse a hard delete of the winner of a non-undone merge unless forced.
**Fix.** `mergeEvents` locks both event rows in id order (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE`) first; the automatic path is a stricter wrapper (no corrections or open reports on either side, equal start times, same country), the first run is capped (for example 20 clusters) and reported before the cap is lifted, and every automatic merge is tagged `source = exact_repeat` in the journal.

### R4D-8. The derive job overwrites pins without a journal, its order can leave 158 pins permanently untrusted, and `result_type` cannot reach cached pins
**Spine:** AD-14 449-491, 518-525; AD-39 3567-3599 (table 3578-3586).
**Failure.**
1. *No rollback for the pin itself.* Step 4 "re-geocodes open events with the cleaned query" (about 270 calls) and "reverse-geocodes the 158 pins" (the analysis totals about 430 calls, roughly 14% of the 3,000-credit daily free tier in `docs/infrastructure/5-geolocation.md`; cache hits are free). The stated rollback is "set the columns back to NULL", which restores nothing: the re-geocode replaces `location_details`, `latitude`, `longitude` and, through the writer, `timezone`, and a timezone change moves the event's absolute time for users who added it to the calendar. Keep `place_derive_log (schedule_id, run_id, before, after)` (customJsonb) or run the re-geocode as a dry-run table applied behind a flag. The reverse-geocode for the 158 must write **only** `countryCode` (and its provenance), never the mapper's whole `LocationDetails` (it would replace the formatted address and confidence with the nearest-address answer).
2. *Order and selector.* Leg (e) makes "no `countryCode`" untrusted. If `pin_trust` is derived first for those 158 as `untrusted`, the later country-code fill does not re-select them (`pin_trust IS NULL` is false) and the "re-run once area keys exist" pass selects on `pin_evaluated_with` differing, which the first pass wrote identical. Rule: the country-code fill runs first, and `pin_trust` stays **NULL** (pending) while a pin has no `countryCode` yet; `pin_evaluated_with` must record every input absent from the row (floor, gazetteer version, default country, result-type list, "country code present").
3. *`result_type`.* It lives inside the `location_details` jsonb (no DDL), but `geolocation_cache` has no expiry and no version (`cache-store.ts`: `getCached` returns whatever was stored): every cache hit returns the old shape without `result_type` forever. AD-14 Rule 2 says "absence means untrusted", which would turn every cached pin into `untrusted`/AREA; the opposite reading leaves the cap inactive for them. State which, and fold a shape version into the cache key (`buildCacheKey`) so old entries are bypassed (bounded by the Geoapify quota).
4. *Stale inputs.* The expected country depends on the schedule text, the **event** text and `GEOAPIFY_DEFAULT_COUNTRY`. `events.location` is written outside `writeSchedulePlace()` (correction, enrichment), so editing it leaves every schedule's `pin_trust` stale.
5. *Switch gate.* Step 5 enables readers when `area_status IS NULL` count is 0. A pinned schedule written by a straggling old-version Lambda has `pin_trust` NULL and would read as no pin (map link gone). Add `pin_trust IS NULL AND latitude IS NOT NULL` and `country_key IS NULL` to the gate.
6. *Row-count ratchet.* New tables gain rows only if tests leave them; the derive job's tests need cleanup like any other. An advisory lock (R4D-4) leaves no row, unlike a lease table (compare `vendor_call_locks`, which the harness had to sweep).

## Medium

### R4D-9. `member_count`, `place_count`, `concurrent`: writer coverage, the hard-delete hook and the definition of "distinct places"
**Spine:** 2483-2484, 2938-2943, 2659-2662.
- *`deleted_at` writers outside the ratchet.* `events.deleted_at` is written at `merge-events.ts:221, 278`, `resolvers.ts:1690` (auto soft-delete at the cancelled-report threshold), `1763` (dismissing reports restores), `1798` and `1814` (`restoreEvent`), and `1948` (hard delete). The ratchet scans writers of `group_id`, `member_count`, `status`, `anchor_event_id`, `confirmed_at`; a seventh path that sets `deleted_at` will not call the recount. Extend the source scan to `deletedAt` on `events`, or compute `member_count` at read for the displayed rows.
- *Hard delete.* The "pre-delete hook" counts the doomed row. Delete, then recount in the same transaction.
- *Inputs that change without a membership change.* `place_count` and `concurrent` depend on schedules (enrichment's `mergeSchedules` inserts and deletes, corrections edit them), on place text and on the gazetteer; none of those writers call the group writer. A weekly run that gains a date flips `concurrent`; a gazetteer update changes `place_count`. Stamp the aggregates (`derived_with`, like `pin_evaluated_with`), recompute on membership change and nightly, and call `recount` from `writeSchedulePlace`/`mergeSchedules` for grouped events.
- *`isSamePlace` is not an equivalence relation* (three values, similar names): "distinct places among active members" needs a defined clustering (union-find over `same`, each `unresolved` counted separately) or the count depends on member order. `concurrent` ("all date ranges overlap") should use each member's span, state that a member with two disjoint schedules spans the gap, and use pairwise overlap in 1-D (equal to a common intersection).
- *Which members count.* After R4D-1 only strong members are hidden, so the label counts come from them (`strong_count`, places of strong members).

### R4D-10. Tag evidence: the "only event of its post" input exists, the evidence is volatile, and the measurement has look-ahead
**Spine:** 2765-2783.
- The spine says "only event of their post cannot be read from `events_in_post` at ingestion". It can: `posts.extracted_event_count` is written at extraction (`process-ai-job.ts` 7.5, `extractedEventCount: events.length`) before the events are enqueued (step 8), so it is available to every sibling message regardless of arrival order. Use it (it is the pre-split count; a place-split post counts 1 and its siblings are grouped by the split anyway). Without it, the first sibling of a three-event post sees one linked event and passes the test while its siblings fail, an arrival-order-dependent join through a roundup's tags. In-sample exposure is nil (0 of 7 tag-zone pairs touch a multi-event post) but 157 of 1,467 events (10.7%) are in multi-event posts, so it is forward risk.
- Record the shared tag and the post id in the join journal (R4D-5); a later primary-post change does not re-evaluate, harmless once tag joins are `link` (R4D-1), harmful if they collapse.
- "Not a mall or city tag": the list is not defined; if it is "df above the cut" the rare test already covers it; if it is a curated list it is another stored, versioned fact.

### R4D-11. Split, group and country: the deterministic group id, a dissolved group, and what a child copies
**Spine:** 2949-2951, AD-38 3245-3250, 3260-3261, 3294-3295.
- *The id has no country.* `UUID v5(source post, k)` plus `ON CONFLICT (id) DO NOTHING`: whichever sibling arrives first creates the group and fixes its country; siblings in another country are not attached. With a Jakarta/Bandung/Singapore split, if the Singapore sibling lands first the two Indonesian siblings are excluded. Include the country key in the v5 input: one group per (post, k, country).
- *Late sibling after a dissolve.* After a moderator dissolves the group, `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` finds the row and a late sibling would attach to a dissolved group (or silently undo the dissolve). The writer checks `status` and leaves the sibling ungrouped.
- *A split whose children end in one country each* can leave groups with one member (dormant rows with names). Create the group only when at least two same-country children exist.
- *`INSERT ... SELECT` copy list.* New columns must be classified in the unit test that enumerates `getTableConfig(events).columns`: `notify_due_at` reset to NULL (otherwise a child of an event still inside its 90 s window copies a due time and an unset `notified_at`), `group_join` reset with `group_id`, `country_key` recomputed from the child's own place.
- *Event-posts ordinal copy.* Secondary links copy with `siblingOrdinal(m, j)` too, not only the primary; a null manual ordinal stays null.

### R4D-12. What the AD-38 Rule 5 simplification (about ten events, moderator action instead of a proposals table) loses
**Spine:** 3316-3325, 3226-3273.
- *Preview.* The dry run is the only place a moderator sees what a split will do (children, ordinals, favorites to copy, calendar entries moved, group). `undoEventSplit` is refused with `SPLIT_DIVERGED` as soon as a child gains a favorite or an enrichment, so "undo" is a minutes-long window. Keep a mandatory `dryRun` in the helper (and in the mutation).
- *Idempotency and concurrency.* A double click or two moderators: the second request names schedule ids that moved. The helper must take the expected schedule ids (or an `updated_at` token) and refuse a mismatch. The rule "a null return from `insertEventWithPrimaryPost` is an error to retry, never already represented" loops forever on a split that already happened (the slot is held by its own child). Return the existing result when the slot's event is the journaled child.
- *The residual six (place only in schedule titles).* Without a model call the moderator has to supply the partition (schedule to place); say so, and the contributor-key discussion becomes moot. A 28-second API Lambda should not host a model call.
- *Duplicate check.* Step 2 of Rule 5 (every backfill-created event passes `findMatchingEvent`) disappears; a child may duplicate a live single-place event of the same series. Have the split report possible duplicates per child (do not auto-merge).
- *Gate.* Rule 5 still lists the AD-36 Rule 10 gate, which the sweep alone cannot meet (68% of pairs are stub pairs). Say whether a moderator split is gated.
- *Standing drift query* (events with more than one place and an open schedule) stays as a nightly consistency query; it costs nothing and is the only way to see unsplit events created by an old Lambda alias.
- *Cheaper:* a script with a dry-run default like `src/merge-events-by-slug.ts`, run by the owner on the ten, avoids a new mutation, UI and authorization surface.

### R4D-13. `event_groups` schema details that will bite at migration time
**Spine:** 2464-2490, 2959-2967.
- Rule 1's enum list omits `series_phrase` (`group_source_enum` "gains `place_split` and `series_stem`"), but the detector table uses it. All `group_*` enums are new, so every value must be in the first `CREATE TYPE`; adding one later is `ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE`, which cannot be used by a backfill in the same drizzle migration batch.
- `event_groups.name` nullability is not stated; `groupName` can return `''` (R4D-5), and Rule 1 lists `name` last without a type.
- `stem` stores a pair-dependent core (R4D-5); define it as the canonical token string of the first pair's cores and compare members with `core(name, ownPlace, stemText)`.
- Rule 1's text lists the columns `stem`, `keyword`, `confirmed_at`, `member_count`, `place_count`, `concurrent` but not the three added by this review (`group_join`, `country_key` on both tables, `strong_count`); the schema paragraph is the one place a builder reads, so the amendment must edit it, not only the rules below.

## Low

### R4D-14. Event identity (ordinals) shares a normalizer that will be tuned
**Spine:** 2799-2802 (`normalizeName()` is the only normalizer; `assignExtractionOrdinals` "use them"), AD-38 3215-3225.
`assignExtractionOrdinals` sorts by earliest date, then its own `normalizeEventName` (trim, lower-case, collapse spaces; `assign-extraction-ordinals.ts:45-47`), then response index. Replacing it with `normalizeName()` (diacritics, punctuation, connectors) changes which names tie, and a tie falls to the extractor's non-deterministic response index, so a re-extraction of an existing post can swap two events' ordinals and enrich the wrong event. `j` in `siblingOrdinal` also orders by "normalized venue name". Freeze an `ordinalSortKey()` (v1) with a snapshot test; `normalizeName()` (diacritic folding today, more later) is tuned for matching and must not feed identity. The same holds for the gazetteer version inside `isSamePlace`, which decides how many places a re-extracted event has and therefore which slots exist.

### R4D-15. Legacy events have `notified_at IS NULL`, so "a split never notifies" is not true for them
**Spine:** 2912-2915, AD-38 3249, 3294.
Migration 0065 added `notified_at` without a backfill; events ingested before it are NULL. A child copies that NULL, and AD-30 Rule 10 sends "exactly one notification" when a later primary change makes an event organizer-authored. A split of a legacy event therefore multiplies that future notification by the number of children. At split time stamp `notified_at = now()` on the original and every child when NULL (and say that `notify_due_at` is reset, R4D-11).

### R4D-16. Conventions: hand-written constraints, soft-delete wording, jsonb, tooling
- *drizzle-kit.* Everything in R4D-3 and R4D-6 (composite FK with a column-list `SET NULL`, the `LEAST/GREATEST` expression index, the `CHECK`s, the partial indexes on `events.group_id` and the series lookup) is hand-written SQL that drizzle-kit 0.21.4 cannot express; the schema builder keeps an approximation (for the FK, `onDelete: 'set null'` so `getTablesInDeleteOrder` skips it), and a header comment names the migration, as `schedules.oneMainPerEventIdx` does.
- *Soft delete.* project-context requires `deletedAt` on removable tables and a partial index on that condition. `event_groups` uses `status = 'dissolved'` instead and nulls members' `group_id`. That is a deliberate deviation (a dissolved group has members in a journal, not rows to hide), so say so in Rule 1, and add the partial index `event_groups (id) WHERE status = 'active'` only if the Rule 13 join plan needs it.
- *jsonb.* Any id list in the new journals (dissolved members, moved schedule ids, repointed calendar additions, `place_derive_log` before/after) must be `customJsonb` (ratchet `jsonb-columns.test.ts`), following `event_merges`; use `uuid[]` where the elements are plain ids and no SQL reads inside.
- *Tooling.* `getTablesInDeleteOrder` skips only self-references today; the planned skip of set-null edges must see the composite FK as set-null (above). The row-count ratchet needs the group tests to clean up and `seed.ts` to recreate the system user.

## Areas checked and found sound

- **The advisory lock itself.** Transaction-scoped, the ingestion transaction makes no external call, the parameter types under the unnamed-statement path and the lock is released at rollback (scratch run); only the key and the order of locks need change (R4D-4).
- **Phrase score as a collapse test.** Identical cores score 1.00 independent of the weights, so the collapse tier is reproducible; the `MIN_SHARED_TOKENS` guard caps single-token identical cores at 0.5 (they never group), which is the safe direction. 28 of 30 components at the adopted bar are identical-only.
- **Favorites copy at split.** `favorites` is unique `(user_id, event_id)` including soft-deleted rows; copying to a new child cannot collide, and the copy with the source `created_at` is idempotent with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`. Undo by invariant must upsert (a soft-deleted row exists), not insert.
- **`calendar_additions` repoint.** Both pointers (`schedule_id`, `event_id`) and the journal field match the schema and the four event-level readers; the consistency query in AD-38 Rule 4 is the right guard.
- **Ordinal formula.** `siblingOrdinal(k, j) = k + 100 j` with the post cap of 99 and `smallint` range (j up to 327) is collision-free given the unconditional unique indexes; the remaining risk is only the normalizer coupling (R4D-14).
- **Expand/derive/switch/contract order** of AD-39 Rule 7 is sound in outline (nullable columns, NULL = not derived, jobs not migrations, enum values not used in the same release, journal `when` check); the findings above are about inputs and gates, not about the sequence.
- **Exact-repeat merge as `system-dedup`** reuses journaled, undoable `mergeEvents`; favorites, calendar additions, reports, `event_posts` (never deleted) and aliases are handled by the existing code (verified in `merge-events.ts`); the issues are the missing locks, corrections, schedule match and group state (R4D-7).
- **Same-poster stub signal.** Arithmetic ceiling 0.60 < `high` 0.75, applied only to same-account pairs; it cannot auto-merge on its own.
- **`posts.hashtags` is `text[]` with a GIN index**, not jsonb; no `customJsonb` question arises for the tag evidence.
- **The unordered-pair index as a concept** (no re-queue of a rejected pair) works as designed on the scratch database once the live insert target and the pre-check are handled (R4D-6).
