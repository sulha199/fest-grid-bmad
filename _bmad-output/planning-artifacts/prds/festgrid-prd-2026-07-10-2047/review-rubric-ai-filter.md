# PRD Quality Review — FestDaily (AI Prompt-Based Custom Event Filter update)

Scope: this pass weights the NEW/CHANGED content only — §3.1's added sentence, §3.15, §4.3's new fields (city/province/adminArea/venueType), §4.16's changed `filters` type, §4.18 (EventFilterInput), §4.19 (AIEventFilter), §8.4, §8.5 — with the rest of the document read only for cross-reference accuracy. Pre-existing, already-flagged document-wide issues (no Glossary/ID scheme, no UJs anywhere, the 3.4–3.6 numbering drift, Security boilerplate opening clause, Section 2 vision-theater phrasing) are not re-litigated per `.memlog.md`.

## Overall verdict

The new schema work (§4.18/4.19) is clean and well-motivated — the shared `EventFilterInput` shape is a real consolidation, not busywork, and the "Considered and rejected" callout in §3.15 is genuine trade-off reasoning rather than theater. But §3.15's prose oversells what the schema actually delivers: the flagship example in its own opening sentence ("this weekend") cannot be represented by the `DateAnchor`/`DayOfWeek` grammar it ships with, and the proximity-radius half of the "locked vocabulary" has no stated resolution mechanism at all (region-mode gets one, proximity-mode doesn't). Layered on top, the feature is silent on who funds the underlying Gemini call and whether it's gated the way every other AI-touching surface in this PRD explicitly is — a real decision left unmade in a document that otherwise makes exactly these calls explicitly. These are fixable in an hour of PM attention, but as written an engineer could not build §3.15 to spec without guessing on at least two of its stated capabilities.

## Decision-readiness — thin

The `> Considered and rejected` callout is a genuine example of the rubric's ask: it names a real alternative (open-dialogue chatbot), states what it would cost (BYOK key reused as a free chat proxy — an abuse vector), and explains why the narrower design was chosen instead. That's the right shape.

Where it falls down is on the decisions the section *doesn't* make. Every other AI-touching surface in this PRD is explicit about funding and gating: §3.7 spells out BYOK-only extraction, the Tier 1/Tier 2 fairness algorithm, and even a narrowly-scoped system-key fallback for one specific sub-case (AI-Assisted Location Inference). §3.15 introduces a third Gemini-consuming code path — the prompt-to-filter extraction call — and says nothing about whose key pays for it, or whether the feature is available to a user with no BYOK key at all (including the unauthenticated visitors §3.1's Discovery page presumably serves, since §3.15 is framed as "a faster alternative to operating the manual controls in Section 3.1"). Given how carefully the rest of the document treats this exact question, its absence here reads as a dodged decision rather than an oversight of no consequence.

### Findings
- **high** AI Filter's API-key funding and tier-gating is unaddressed (§3.15) — Section 3.7/3.8 establish an elaborate BYOK/quota-fairness model for every other Gemini call in the product, including a narrowly-scoped system-key carve-out for one sub-case. Section 3.15 adds a third Gemini call (prompt→filter extraction) and never states whose key funds it or whether a user without a BYOK key (or an unauthenticated Discovery visitor) can use the feature at all. *Fix:* add one bullet stating the funding source (subscriber's own BYOK key? new system-funded allowance? gated to `contributing_user` only?) and whether unauthenticated visitors can invoke it.
- **low** No `> Deferred to implementation` callout in §3.15 despite open items — §3.13 and §3.14 both use this callout for exactly this kind of loose end (dedup strategy, rate-limiting, ownership verification). §3.15 has at least three comparable open items (key funding/gating above, the weekend/proximity gaps below) but uses no such callout, breaking the pattern the document itself established two sections earlier. *Fix:* either resolve the open items or flag them explicitly, matching sibling sections' convention.

## Substance over theater — adequate

No theater in the new material. The rejected-alternatives paragraph and the "Summary Is the Only Transparency Layer" bullet ("This single mechanism is deliberately the entire trust/transparency layer") are both real design commitments with a stated cost, not filler. No new NFR boilerplate was added for this feature.

## Strategic coherence — strong

§3.15 is explicitly tied to the Engagement goal ("lowering the effort to reach a precise result"), matching the pattern already used for §3.13 (Acquisition) and §3.14 (Acquisition) rather than adding a free-floating capability. The MVP scope logic — single-shot, no chat memory, no edit UI, narrow price vocabulary deferred to §8.5 — reads as a deliberately constrained v1 rather than a backlog dump.

## Done-ness clarity — broken

This is where the update is weakest, and it's the dimension the rubric says to be least forgiving on.

1. **The section's own example breaks its own schema.** §3.15 opens with "free jazz events in Kota Yogyakarta *this weekend*" as the motivating example. `EventFilterInput.dateRange` (§4.18) is a `DateRangeFilter{anchor: DateAnchor, offsetAmount, offsetUnit}` where `DateAnchor` is only `TODAY | THIS_WEEK | THIS_MONTH` — a contiguous window anchored at the current period, not a sub-range. `dayOfWeek` is a single `DayOfWeek`, not a set. Neither construct, nor any combination of them, can express "Saturday and Sunday of this week" — there is no `WEEKEND` anchor and no way to select two days at once. An engineer implementing this literally cannot satisfy the PRD's own headline example.
2. **Proximity-mode has no extraction mechanism.** The "Locked Filter Vocabulary" bullet lists "location (proximity radius or admin-area/region text match)" as promptable. §3.15's "Region Matching" bullet then explains, in detail, how region-mode resolves (existing geolocation provider's admin-area data, with a named accuracy caveat). No equivalent explanation exists for proximity-mode: `LocationFilter.coordinates`/`radiusMeters` (§4.18) are documented as reusing "existing coordinates + radius, Section 3.3" — but Section 3.3's radius search is populated from an explicit UI action (GPS or map pick), not free text. Nothing in §3.15 says how a prompt like "jazz events near me" or "within 5km of downtown" is supposed to turn into a `Coordinates` value. This is a testable-consequence gap in the truest sense: there's no way to write an acceptance test for proximity-mode extraction from the current text.

### Findings
- **high** Flagship example unresolvable by the shipped schema (§3.15 intro, §4.18 `DateRangeFilter`/`DayOfWeek`) — "this weekend" cannot be constructed from `{TODAY,THIS_WEEK,THIS_MONTH}` + offset, nor from a single `DayOfWeek`. *Fix:* either add a `WEEKEND` anchor (and clarify how it composes with `offsetAmount`/`offsetUnit`), change `dayOfWeek` to a set, or swap the example for one the schema actually supports and note the limitation.
- **high** Proximity-mode ("near me" / radius) has no stated resolution mechanism (§3.15, §4.18 `LocationFilter.coordinates`) — region-mode's resolution path is explicit (geolocation provider's admin-area data); proximity-mode's is not addressed at all. *Fix:* state whether the AI resolves a place name via the same geolocation lookup used elsewhere (§3.7's inference pattern is the obvious template), whether it falls back to the user's saved location preference (§3.3) for "near me" phrasing, or whether proximity-mode is out of scope for v1 despite being listed in the locked vocabulary.

## Scope honesty — thin

The two `>` callout conventions used elsewhere in this document (`Deferred to implementation`, `Considered and rejected`) are the right mechanism for exactly the omissions found above, and §3.15 uses the second but not the first. The funding/gating question and the weekend/proximity gaps are the kind of thing a reader is currently meant to infer rather than being told is unresolved — see the Decision-readiness finding above for the specific fix.

## Downstream usability — thin

- `FilterHub` is introduced as a proper noun in the "FilterHub Entry Point" bullet and used again in the rejected-alternatives note, but it is never defined and appears nowhere else in the document (confirmed by full-document search). Section 3.1 — the section §3.15 explicitly extends — describes "a free-text search bar" and "filter events by type and category" but never names the control `FilterHub`. A reader encountering §3.15 in isolation (the rubric's "pulled out alone" test) cannot tell whether `FilterHub` is the same thing as §3.1's search/filter bar, a new component, or existing UI not otherwise documented here.
- Widget's filter surface is now ambiguous after the `EventFilterInput` share. §4.16 changed `Widget.filters` to the full `EventFilterInput` type ("the same typed structure Discovery's manual filters and AI Event Filters ... use"), but §3.14's own prose — unchanged in this pass — still enumerates the widget's configurable dimensions as only "social media account, event type, category, keyword, and location/coordinates + radius." It's now unstated whether Epic 6's widget generator UI is expected to expose the newly-shared `dateRange`, `dayOfWeek`, `venueType`, and `isFree` fields too, or whether the widget deliberately only ever populates a subset of the type it's declared to share. Whoever writes the Epic 6 widget-generator story needs this resolved, not inferred.

### Findings
- **medium** `Widget.filters`/`EventFilterInput` scope mismatch between §3.14 and §4.16 (§3.14, §4.16, §4.18) — §3.14's filter-dimension list wasn't updated to match the fuller shared type now backing `Widget.filters`. *Fix:* either state explicitly that the widget generator only ever populates the pre-existing subset (and the extra fields are simply unused, present for type-reuse only), or update §3.14's list to include venue type / date range / day-of-week / isFree if the generator is meant to expose them.
- **medium** `FilterHub` used as an undefined proper noun (§3.15, two occurrences) — never introduced or tied back to §3.1's search/filter bar. *Fix:* either name the control in §3.1 (or wherever it's actually defined) and cross-reference it from §3.15, or drop the proper noun and describe it structurally ("the existing filter bar").
- **low** Cross-reference mismatch: "same pattern as Favorites/Subscriptions, Section 3.2" (§3.15, "Saving and Reuse" bullet) — Section 3.2 (Personalization Features) covers Favorite Events and the Favorites/Added-Events pages only; Subscriptions are documented in Section 3.7, not 3.2. *Fix:* either drop "/Subscriptions" from the parenthetical or cite 3.7 alongside 3.2.
- **low** `dateRange` vs. `dayOfWeek` combinability unstated (§4.18 `EventFilterInput`) — `LocationFilter`'s doc comment explicitly states proximity-mode and region-mode are "not both at once"; no equivalent statement exists for whether `dateRange` and `dayOfWeek` can be populated together from one prompt (e.g. "next week, on Fridays"). *Fix:* add a one-line comment stating whether the two compose or are mutually exclusive, matching `LocationFilter`'s precedent.

## Shape fit — adequate, with a caveat

FestDaily is a consumer product where the document-wide absence of UJs is already flagged and out of scope for this pass. Worth noting, though: §3.15 is meaningfully more interaction-heavy than its two sibling additions (§3.13 voting and §3.14 widget embedding are largely CRUD/backend-shaped; §3.15 introduces a new free-text input paradigm with an overlay, a collapsed-summary row, and an AI round-trip). Section 3.12 (Global UI & Navigation Patterns) already defines a blocking-overlay loader pattern for "data extraction" actions — the natural fit for an in-flight Gemini call — but §3.15 never cites it, leaving the loading state during prompt resolution unspecified. This doesn't rise to reopening the document-wide UJ gap as a finding on its own, but it is one concrete way the missing-UJs pattern costs more here than it did for the two prior additions.

### Findings
- **low** No loading-state cross-reference for the AI extraction round-trip (§3.15) — Section 3.12 already defines a blocking full-screen-overlay pattern for data-extraction actions; §3.15 doesn't say whether prompt resolution uses it, a lighter-weight overlay-scoped spinner, or something else. *Fix:* one clause tying the prompt-resolution wait state to §3.12's existing pattern (or explicitly carving out a different one).
